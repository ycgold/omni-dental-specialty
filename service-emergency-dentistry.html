Overview

What Is a
Dental Emergency?

Many Oxnard patients want to know what counts as a dental emergency and what to do first. Cleveland Clinic describes a knocked out tooth as a dental emergency. Cleveland Clinic's toothache page says unbearable tooth pain along with fever and chills means you are having a dental emergency.

The American Dental Association's MouthHealthy site says to call your dentist and describe your condition in as much detail as possible. If you are unsure what to do, call our office and tell us what is happening.

This page is general information and cannot diagnose a problem. At Omni Dental Specialty in Oxnard, your dentist talks through what you are noticing and explains the options.

What to Consider

Why Oxnard Patients Ask About Dental Emergencies

01
A Knocked Out Tooth

MouthHealthy says to keep the tooth moist at all times. Cleveland Clinic says to hold it by the crown, not the root, and to store it in milk, not water, if you cannot put it back.

02
A Severe Toothache

MouthHealthy says to rinse with warm water, floss to remove trapped food and not put aspirin on the aching tooth or gum tissues. Cleveland Clinic says a severe toothache needs professional dental treatment.

03
A Cracked or Broken Tooth

MouthHealthy says to rinse your mouth with warm water to clean the area and apply cold compresses to your face to reduce swelling. MedlinePlus says to contact a dentist right away if a tooth is broken or knocked out, and to bring the tooth if you can find it.

04
A Bitten Tongue or Lip

MouthHealthy says to clean the area with water and apply a cold compress. Tell your dentist about any injury to your mouth.

05
Something Stuck Between Teeth

MouthHealthy says to try to remove it with floss and not to use sharp or pointed instruments.

06
Infection and Swelling

The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research says that if a tooth becomes infected, an abscess, or pocket of pus, may form, causing pain, facial swelling and fever. MedlinePlus says to contact your dentist for a throbbing toothache that does not go away.

Good to Know

When to Call 911 or Go to an Emergency Room

These general points come from health organizations and can help Oxnard patients decide where to go. Your dentist explains what applies to your situation. You can also see all of our dental services in Oxnard.

  1. 01

    Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room

    If you believe you are experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

  2. 02

    Pain with fever and chills

    Cleveland Clinic says unbearable tooth pain along with fever and chills means a dental emergency, and that you should call a dentist or go to the nearest emergency room right away. It adds that an infection in your mouth can spread to other areas of your body.

  3. 03

    Swelling, a knot or a fever

    Cleveland Clinic lists swelling below your eye, a knot on your jaw and a fever over 101 degrees Fahrenheit among the reasons to go to an emergency room. MedlinePlus calls a swollen area of the jaw a very serious symptom of a tooth abscess.

  4. 04

    Bleeding or pain that will not ease

    Cleveland Clinic also lists bleeding that will not stop with applied pressure, and unbearable tooth pain that does not improve with medication, as reasons to go to an emergency room.

  5. 05

    A possible broken jaw

    MedlinePlus says that if you cannot close your upper and lower teeth together, your jaw may be broken. It says this requires medical help right away at a dentist's office or hospital.

  6. 06

    After a fall or injury

    Cleveland Clinic says to contact a dentist or go to an emergency room as soon as possible after a tooth is knocked out. After a hard fall, it says a provider may also check for a concussion.

Your Options

What the Choices Can Look Like

Cleveland Clinic says treatment options depend on how severe the problem is. These are general descriptions only.

01
A Knocked Out Tooth

Cleveland Clinic says a dentist checks that the tooth is correctly positioned and splints it to the neighboring teeth. If the tooth cannot be saved, it says a dentist may recommend a partial denture, a dental bridge or a dental implant.

02
A Damaged Tooth

Cleveland Clinic lists fillings for small cavities or minor breaks and crowns for larger cavities or fractures.

03
An Infected Tooth

Cleveland Clinic says root canal therapy is used when infection reaches the tooth pulp, and that a tooth too damaged to save may be removed.

Before Your Visit

Questions to Ask When You Call or Visit

Use this list to get ready, and add questions of your own. The topics follow information shared by Cleveland Clinic and MouthHealthy. If you want to ask something before you come to our Oxnard office, contact us.

When you call about a problem

  • What is happening, and when did it start?
  • Is there swelling, bleeding or a fever?
  • Was a tooth knocked out, and do I have it with me?
  • Should I call 911 or go to an emergency room instead?
  • How should I protect the tooth or the area until I am seen?
  • How does your office handle urgent concerns?

Please do not send medical details through this website.

About your visit and follow up

  • What will the exam include, and will I need an X-ray?
  • What is causing the problem, and what are my options?
  • What does each option involve?
  • Which follow up visits will I need?
  • What should I do if something feels wrong after my visit?
  • What will it cost, and what does my insurance plan cover?

Cleveland Clinic says dentists often take a dental X-ray, so it is fair to ask what it is for.
