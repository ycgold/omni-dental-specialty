Overview

What Are
Wisdom Teeth?

Many Oxnard patients ask whether their wisdom teeth need to come out. The American Dental Association's MouthHealthy site says wisdom teeth, also called third molars, usually come in between ages 17 and 21. It says they have that name because they come in at a more mature age.

MouthHealthy says wisdom teeth can become impacted, meaning trapped in the jaw or under the gums, when there is not enough room or they grow in the wrong position. The American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons (AAOMS) says a tooth that cannot fully enter the mouth is usually called impacted.

MouthHealthy says that before deciding what to do, a dentist examines your mouth and takes an X-ray. At Omni Dental Specialty in Oxnard, your dentist talks through what the exam shows and explains your options.

This page is general information and cannot tell you whether your wisdom teeth need attention. We welcome patients from Oxnard, El Rio and College Park. You can read about our Oxnard dental office or contact us to ask a question.

What to Consider

Why Oxnard Patients Ask About Wisdom Teeth

01
Trapped Food

MouthHealthy says teeth in the wrong position can trap food, which can promote bacteria that cause cavities. Your dentist explains whether this applies to you.

02
Hard to Clean

MouthHealthy says it can be difficult to floss between partially erupted teeth and the neighboring molars. Ask your dentist how to clean the area.

03
Infection and Swelling

MouthHealthy says partially erupted teeth can let bacteria into the gums, which may cause infection, pain, swelling and jaw stiffness. Tell your dentist about any of these.

04
Crowding or Damage

MouthHealthy says wisdom teeth can cause crowding or damage to neighboring teeth. Your dentist can look at the area and explain what they see.

05
Cysts

MouthHealthy says cysts can damage tooth roots or destroy supporting bone. AAOMS says cysts or tumors can damage adjacent teeth or nearby structures.

06
Signs to Notice

AAOMS lists tender or bleeding gums, red or swollen gums, jaw pain or swelling, bad breath or an unpleasant taste and difficulty opening the mouth. It says these can come from poorly cleaned wisdom teeth or from infection around impacted teeth.

The Process

How Wisdom Tooth Removal Is Described, Step by Step

These are general stages described by health organizations, so Oxnard patients know what to ask when wisdom teeth come up.

Step 01
Exam and X-ray

MouthHealthy says the dentist examines your mouth and takes an X-ray before deciding on treatment. Tell your dentist about any pain or changes you have noticed.

Step 02
Talking Through Options

MouthHealthy says you and your dentist can discuss the course of treatment together. AAOMS describes options that range from removal to watching the teeth over time, called active surveillance.

Step 03
Deciding Whether to Remove

MedlinePlus lists impacted teeth that are causing problems, such as wisdom teeth, among the reasons for removing a tooth. It also lists deep infection, overcrowding and gum disease.

Step 04
Numbing the Area

MedlinePlus says tooth removal starts with a local anesthetic that numbs the area, and the tooth is then loosened and taken out. [TBD: confirm with client which treatment steps take place at our office]

Step 05
Healing at Home

MedlinePlus says the socket takes 1 to 2 weeks to heal, and that the surrounding bone and tissue may take longer. It advises not drinking from a straw for at least 24 hours.

Step 06
Contacting Your Dentist

MedlinePlus lists fever or chills, severe swelling, pus, and pain or bleeding that continues several hours after the procedure as reasons to call your dentist. Ask your dentist what to watch for.

Good to Know

A Closer Look at Wisdom Teeth Care

These general points can help Oxnard patients prepare for a conversation with their dentist. Your dentist explains what applies to your situation. You can also see all of our dental services in Oxnard.

  1. 01

    If wisdom teeth stay in place

    MouthHealthy says wisdom teeth that are not removed should still be monitored, because problems can develop later. It advises flossing around them and visiting the dentist regularly.

  2. 02

    Pain is not the only sign

    AAOMS says a tooth without pain can still have disease. This is one reason regular checkups matter even when nothing hurts.

  3. 03

    When to see your dentist

    MouthHealthy says some discomfort can be normal as wisdom teeth come in, and that pain is a reason to see your dentist. Tell your dentist what you notice and when it started.

  4. 04

    Things to avoid after removal

    MedlinePlus lists the blood clot falling out of the socket days after the procedure as a risk. It also says not to smoke or use tobacco.

  5. 05

    Cold compresses and soft foods

    MedlinePlus says to apply a cold compress to the cheek for 10 to 20 minutes at a time. It also says to avoid hard and crunchy foods for 1 week.

  6. 06

    Questions to bring with you

    Write down what you want to know before you come in. The checklist further down this page groups questions by topic. Your dentist answers in plain language.

Your Options

What the Choices Can Look Like

AAOMS describes options that range from removal to watching the teeth over time. These are general descriptions only.

01
Watching the Teeth

AAOMS says wisdom teeth that have come in, work normally, cause no pain and have no cavities may not need to be removed. These teeth still need regular cleanings, checkups and periodic X-rays.

02
Removal

MouthHealthy says removal may be needed when there is pain, infection, cysts, damage to neighboring teeth, gum disease or decay that cannot reasonably be restored. Your dentist explains whether any of these apply to you.

03
Removal as Part of Other Care

MouthHealthy says dentists may also recommend removing wisdom teeth as part of braces or other dental care. Ask your dentist how your wisdom teeth fit with your overall plan.

Before Your Visit

Questions to Ask at Your Visit

Bring this list to your visit and add your own. The topics follow information shared by MouthHealthy, AAOMS and MedlinePlus. If you want to ask something before you come to our Oxnard office, contact us.

About your wisdom teeth

  • Are my wisdom teeth impacted, and how do you know?
  • Do I need an X-ray, and what is it for?
  • Are my wisdom teeth affecting my neighboring teeth or gums?
  • Which of my medications or health conditions should we discuss?
  • Do you recommend removing them or watching them over time?
  • If I keep them, how often should they be checked?
