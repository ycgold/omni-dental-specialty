Overview

What Is a
Dental Implant?

A dental implant replaces the root of a missing tooth. Cleveland Clinic describes a threaded post placed in the jawbone that acts like an artificial tooth root, with a small connector called an abutment that extends slightly beyond the gums. The visible tooth on top, such as a crown, a bridge or an implant supported denture, is the restoration.

The American Dental Association's MouthHealthy site says treatment generally has three stages. The implant is placed surgically in the jaw, the bone grows around it during a healing period, and the replacement tooth is attached afterward. Cleveland Clinic calls this fusing of the jawbone to the implant osseointegration.

Implants are one option among several for replacing a missing tooth. The American Academy of Implant Dentistry says each patient's situation differs and that an initial consultation should produce a treatment plan. At Omni Dental Specialty in Oxnard, your dentist reviews your history and talks through which approach may fit you.

This page is general information. Cleveland Clinic says a dentist is the only person who can tell you whether you are a candidate, so we begin with an exam and a conversation. We welcome patients from Oxnard, El Rio and College Park. You can read about our Oxnard dental office or contact us to ask about a consultation.

What to Consider

Why Oxnard Patients Ask About Implants

01
Placed in the Jawbone

Cleveland Clinic describes the post as sitting in the jawbone and acting like an artificial tooth root. The restoration connects to the post through the abutment.

02
Neighboring Teeth

Cleveland Clinic notes that a single tooth implant does not involve the neighboring teeth, while a traditional bridge requires shaving them down. Your dentist explains how this applies to you.

03
A Custom Crown

For a single tooth implant, MouthHealthy says your dentist customizes a new tooth called a dental crown. Your dentist discusses the details at the consultation.

04
More Than One Tooth

MouthHealthy says that when several teeth are missing, custom bridges or dentures are made to fit your mouth and your implants. Your dentist explains the options.

05
Your Health History

Cleveland Clinic says a provider reviews your medical history carefully before suggesting implants. MouthHealthy says chronic conditions such as diabetes or leukemia may interfere with healing.

06
Ongoing Care

Cleveland Clinic points to brushing, flossing and regular dental visits as part of caring for implants. Your dentist explains what ongoing care looks like for you.

The Process

How Implants Work, Step by Step

We walk you through each stage before any treatment begins so you know what to expect from your implant consultation in Oxnard.

Step 01
Consultation and Exam

We review your dental and health history and examine your mouth. Cleveland Clinic names enough healthy jawbone as the main requirement for an implant to take hold.

Step 02
Treatment Planning

The American Academy of Implant Dentistry says a consultation should produce a treatment plan with estimated timing. We explain what each appointment involves and what to expect between visits.

Step 03
Preparing the Jawbone

Cleveland Clinic says a provider may need to place bone grafts to build up places where there is bone loss. Your dentist tells you whether this applies to you.

Implant Placement

Step 06
Attaching the New Tooth

Cleveland Clinic says the restoration is added once the implant has healed. Your dentist explains what your final tooth involves, such as a crown, a bridge or a denture.

Good to Know

A Closer Look at Implant Care

These general points can help Oxnard patients prepare for a consultation. Your dentist explains what applies to your situation. You can also see all of our dental services in Oxnard.

  1. 01

    What your dentist reviews first

    Cleveland Clinic says a provider reviews your medical history carefully before suggesting implants. Your visit also includes a look at your mouth and time for your questions.

  2. 02

    Gum health and jawbone

    Cleveland Clinic lists active or untreated gum disease and poor oral hygiene among the reasons implants may not suit someone. Your dentist explains whether either applies to you.

  3. 03

    Tobacco and healing

    Cleveland Clinic says implants may not be a good idea if you smoke or vape, because this can disrupt healing. Tell your dentist about any tobacco use so you can talk it through.

  4. 04

    Looking after your teeth around an implant

    Cleveland Clinic points to brushing, flossing and regular dental visits. The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research notes that implants placed in the jaw can sometimes lead to inflammation of the tissues around the implant. Tell your dentist about any change in the area.

  5. 05

    Dentures and implants

    MedlinePlus says implants help stabilize a denture and minimize its movement. Your dentist explains whether this is an option for you.

  6. 06

    Questions to bring with you

    Write down what you want to know before you come in. The checklist further down this page groups questions by topic. Your dentist answers in plain language.

Your Options

What the Choices Can Look Like

Cleveland Clinic says implants vary by the type of material, the procedure and the number of teeth they replace. These are general descriptions only.

01
One Missing Tooth

Cleveland Clinic describes a single implant that supports a crown. It also says your dentist may make a temporary tooth to wear until you heal.

02
Several Missing Teeth

Cleveland Clinic describes an implant bridge, where more than one implant supports a bridge that replaces several teeth. MouthHealthy says bridges and dentures are custom made to fit your mouth and your implants.

03
A Full Arch of Teeth

Cleveland Clinic describes a full arch option, where multiple implants support a full arch of teeth. Your dentist explains whether an option like this applies to you.

Before Your Visit

Questions to Ask at Your Consultation

Bring this list to your visit and add your own. The topics follow information shared by Cleveland Clinic and MouthHealthy. If you want to ask something before you come to our Oxnard office, contact us.

About your health and your mouth

  • Which of my medications or health conditions should we discuss?
  • Could tobacco use affect my healing?
  • Do my gums and teeth need attention first?
  • Is there enough healthy jawbone, and what happens if my bone is thin?
  • Should a physician review my health first?
  • Which option fits me: one tooth, several teeth or a full arch?

Please do not send medical details through this website.

About treatment and aftercare

  • What does my treatment plan include, and how long might each stage take?
  • Will I wear a temporary tooth while I heal?
  • How do I care for the area at home, and which follow up visits will I need?
  • What should I do if something feels wrong, and who do I contact?
  • What training and experience does the dentist have with implant placement?
  • What will it cost, and what does my insurance plan cover?

MedlinePlus says implants should only be placed by a well trained dentist skilled in dental implant placement, so training is a fair question to ask.
