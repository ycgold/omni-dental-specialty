Overview

What Are
Dental Veneers?

Many Oxnard patients ask what dental veneers are. Cleveland Clinic describes them as custom made shells that fit over the front surfaces of your teeth. It also says veneers are cosmetic and do not necessarily strengthen or repair your teeth.

The American Dental Association's MouthHealthy site says veneers can improve the appearance of teeth that are chipped or broken, stained, or crooked or misshapen. It adds that veneers can mask gaps in your smile and differ from crowns because they cover only the front surface of a tooth.

What to Consider

Why Oxnard Patients Ask About Veneers

01
The Front Surface Only

MouthHealthy says veneers cover only the front surface of a tooth rather than the entire tooth structure. Cleveland Clinic says a crown, by contrast, covers the entire tooth to add strength and protection.

02
Several Types

MouthHealthy names porcelain and composite as the two main types. Cleveland Clinic also lists no prep and removable veneers.

03
Enamel Is Removed

MouthHealthy says treatment is not reversible because tooth enamel is removed to place a veneer. Cleveland Clinic says some types, such as no prep or minimal prep, are reversible. Your dentist explains which applies.

04
Healthy Teeth and Gums First

MouthHealthy says your dentist must first treat any dental issues, like decay or gum disease. Cleveland Clinic says veneers are only an option if you do not have extensive cavities and gum disease.

05
Grinding and Clenching

MouthHealthy says veneers may not be a good choice if you clench or grind your teeth, or have a deep overbite. Tell your dentist about these habits.

06
Ongoing Care

MouthHealthy says you can still get cavities under or around a veneer. Cleveland Clinic points to brushing, flossing and regular dental visits.

The Process

How Veneers Work, Step by Step

We walk you through each stage before any treatment begins so you know what to expect from a veneer visit in Oxnard.

Step 01
Consultation and Exam

Cleveland Clinic says your dentist examines your teeth and gums at the first appointment to see whether you are eligible for veneers.

Step 02
Choosing a Type and Shade

MouthHealthy says each process includes picking a color shade to match your teeth. Your dentist helps you make that decision.

Step 03
Preparing the Tooth

For a porcelain veneer, MouthHealthy says your dentist prepares the tooth by removing a small amount of enamel from the front and sides. For a composite veneer, the dentist prepares the tooth, then bonds and sculpts the composite material.

Step 04
Impressions and the Lab

MouthHealthy says an impression of the tooth goes to a dental lab, where a technician custom makes the veneer. Cleveland Clinic says this can take a few weeks. [TBD: confirm with client which treatment steps take place at our office]

Step 05
Fit and Bonding

Cleveland Clinic says that at a second office visit your dentist checks the shape, color and fit, bonds the veneers into place and checks your bite.

Step 06
Getting Used to Them

MouthHealthy says it may take a few days to get used to a veneer. Tell your dentist if your bite does not feel right after placement so it can be corrected.

Good to Know

A Closer Look at Veneer Care

These general points can help Oxnard patients prepare for a visit. Your dentist explains what applies to your situation. You can also see all of our dental services in Oxnard.

  1. 01

    Composite and porcelain compared

    MouthHealthy says composite veneers may require less enamel removal and fewer visits, and are easier to fix if damaged. It says they are not as stain or wear resistant as porcelain.

  2. 02

    Veneers and dental bonding

    Cleveland Clinic describes dental bonding as using a tooth colored composite resin to repair chips, fill gaps or change the shape and color of a tooth. It says that, unlike porcelain veneers, bonding is reversible. Your dentist explains how the two compare for your teeth.

  3. 03

    Veneers and whitening

    MouthHealthy says whitening will not work on caps, veneers, crowns or fillings. Cleveland Clinic says whitening products can scratch veneers, so tell your dentist if you plan to whiten your teeth.

  4. 04

    Everyday habits

    MouthHealthy suggests avoiding biting your fingernails or chewing on hard objects or foods. Cleveland Clinic suggests cutting up hard or crunchy foods and chewing them with your back teeth.

  5. 05

    Stains and daily cleaning

    MouthHealthy says to be cautious with foods and drinks that may stain a veneer, such as coffee, tea or red wine. It also advises brushing with fluoride toothpaste and cleaning between your teeth daily.

  6. 06

    Choosing a licensed provider

    MouthHealthy says veneer services from unlicensed individuals may come with potential risks for infection and nerve damage. It advises checking that your provider is licensed to provide dental services in your state.

Your Options

What the Choices Can Look Like

Cleveland Clinic lists composite, porcelain, no prep and removable veneers. These are general descriptions only.

01
Composite Veneers

MouthHealthy says a composite veneer is made from a tooth colored filling material that is bonded to the tooth. Cleveland Clinic says dental impressions are not needed if you choose composite veneers.

02
Porcelain Veneers

MouthHealthy describes a porcelain veneer as a thin shell custom made to fit on the front surface of your teeth. Cleveland Clinic says your dentist must remove some enamel before placement.

03
No Prep and Removable

Cleveland Clinic says no prep veneers require less enamel removal than traditional veneers but still require some, and are not right for everyone. It says there is little research on removable veneers.

Before Your Visit

Questions to Ask at Your Consultation

Bring this list to your visit and add your own. The topics follow information shared by Cleveland Clinic and MouthHealthy. If you want to ask something before you come to our Oxnard office, contact us.

About your teeth and goals

  • Are veneers an option for my teeth, or should something else come first?
  • Do I have decay or gum disease that needs attention first?
  • Do I clench or grind my teeth, and could that affect veneers?
  • Which type fits my goals: composite, porcelain, no prep or removable?
  • How much enamel would be removed, and can it be reversed for this type?
  • How would veneers compare with bonding or a crown for my teeth?

Please do not send medical details through this website.

About treatment and care

  • What does my treatment plan include, and how many visits should I expect?
  • Will I have a temporary veneer while the lab makes mine?
  • How do I care for veneers at home, and which habits should I avoid?
  • What should I do if a veneer chips, loosens or feels wrong, and who do I contact?
  • Is my provider licensed to provide dental services in this state?
  • What will it cost, and what does my insurance plan cover?

MouthHealthy says veneers are cosmetic, so insurance may not cover them unless they are deemed medically necessary.
