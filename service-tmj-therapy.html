Overview

What Are
TMJ Disorders?

This page is for Oxnard patients who have questions about their jaw. The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research (NIDCR) describes temporomandibular disorders, or TMDs, as a group of more than 30 conditions that cause pain and dysfunction in the jaw joint and the muscles that control jaw movement. It says TMJ refers to the jaw joint itself, and that you have one on each side.

MedlinePlus says the temporomandibular joints connect the lower jaw to the skull. It says TMDs include problems with the jaw joints, problems with the chewing muscles and headaches caused by a TMD, and that many resolve on their own while some become chronic.

Cleveland Clinic lists jaw or face pain, stiffness or locking, and clicking, popping or grinding sounds among the symptoms. NIDCR notes that clicking or popping without pain is common, is considered normal and does not require treatment.

What to Consider

Why Oxnard Patients Ask About TMJ Concerns

01
Jaw Pain

NIDCR says pain in the chewing muscles or jaw joint is the most common symptom of a TMD. It says the pain can also spread to the face or neck.

02
Stiffness or Locking

NIDCR lists jaw stiffness, limited movement and jaw locking among the signs that may point to a TMD.

03
Painful Clicking

NIDCR says painful clicking, popping or grating when you open or close your mouth may point to a TMD, while clicking without pain is common.

04
Ear and Head Symptoms

NIDCR lists ringing in the ears, hearing loss and dizziness among the signs that may point to a TMD, and says TMDs can occur along with headaches.

05
A Change in How Teeth Fit

NIDCR lists a change in how the upper and lower teeth fit together among the signs that may point to a TMD.

06
Clenching and Grinding

Cleveland Clinic lists teeth grinding or clenching among the causes, along with jaw injury, arthritis in the jaw and stress that tightens the jaw.

The Process

What a Jaw Evaluation Can Involve

The order can vary, and each person's evaluation is different. We want you to know what to expect, and you can ask about your jaw at a visit to our Oxnard office.

Step 01
Telling Your Story

NIDCR says a clinician takes a detailed history covering where the pain is, when it happens, what triggers it and whether it spreads.

Step 02
Examining the Jaw

MedlinePlus says the head, neck, face and jaw are examined for tenderness, clicking or popping and problems with movement. Cleveland Clinic says a provider may measure how wide you can open your mouth.

Step 03
Imaging When Needed

NIDCR says imaging such as X-ray, MRI or CT may be used.

Step 04
Ruling Out Other Causes

NIDCR says there is no widely accepted standard test for TMDs, and that other conditions may need to be ruled out first.

Step 05
Starting With Simple Steps

NIDCR says many TMDs resolve without treatment and recommends starting with simple, reversible treatments. Its first steps include soft foods, heat or cold and cutting back on clenching, gum chewing and nail biting.

Talking Through Next Steps

Good to Know

A Closer Look at Jaw Health

These general points can help Oxnard patients prepare for a visit. Your dentist explains what applies to your situation. You can also see all of our dental services in Oxnard.

  1. 01

    Symptoms can vary over time

    NIDCR says many TMDs are short lived and resolve on their own, but some become chronic. Cleveland Clinic says TMD can be short term or long lasting.

  2. 02

    Self care comes first

    NIDCR lists eating soft foods, applying heat or cold with jaw stretching and strengthening exercises, and cutting back on clenching, gum chewing and nail biting.

  3. 03

    Medicines

    NIDCR says over the counter NSAIDs such as ibuprofen are one option, and that evidence on how well prescription medicines work is limited.

  4. 04

    Mouth guards and splints

    NIDCR says these appliances fit over the teeth and may help with habits like clenching, but that evidence they reduce pain is limited. It advises making sure an appliance is not designed to alter your bite for good, and stopping to consult a clinician if it causes pain.

  5. 05

    Before any irreversible step

    NIDCR says there is insufficient evidence that bite altering treatments such as crowns, grinding teeth or orthodontics work for TMDs, and that they may make symptoms worse. It advises asking why surgery is suggested, whether other options exist and what the risks are.

  6. 06

    Questions to bring with you

    Write down what you want to know before you come in. The checklist further down this page groups questions by topic. The TMJ Association advises consulting your health care provider about specific treatment options.

Your Options

What Jaw Care Can Look Like

Cleveland Clinic says treatment depends on what is causing your symptoms and how severe they are, and that most people start with noninvasive options. These are general descriptions only.

01
Self Care and Habits

NIDCR describes soft foods, heat or cold, jaw exercises and cutting back on clenching, gum chewing and nail biting. Cleveland Clinic adds good posture and stress management, such as deep breathing or meditation.

02
Appliances and Therapies

Cleveland Clinic lists custom mouth guards or splints, jaw exercises through physical therapy and medicines among the nonsurgical options. NIDCR also describes cognitive behavioral therapy and biofeedback, which uses sensors to help people notice and relax tight jaw muscles.

03
Procedures and Surgery

NIDCR says surgery should be considered only if the joint is destroyed in a way other treatments cannot fix, or if symptoms are severe and persist despite other care. Cleveland Clinic names arthrocentesis, arthroscopy and open joint surgery among the surgical options.

Before Your Visit

Questions to Ask at Your Visit

Bring this list to your visit and add your own. The topics follow information shared by NIDCR and Cleveland Clinic. If you want to ask something before you come to our Oxnard office, contact us.

About your symptoms and your health

  • Where do I feel pain, and when does it happen?
  • Which habits, such as clenching, gum chewing or nail biting, could matter?
  • Could stress or another pain condition be part of this?
  • Which of my medications or health conditions should we discuss?
  • Do I need imaging, and what will it show?
  • Could something else be causing my symptoms?

Please do not send medical details through this website.

About care and next steps

  • Which simple, reversible steps can I try first?
  • What can I do at home, and how long should I try it?
  • If I use a mouth guard or splint, what is it designed to do?
  • Which options are reversible and which are not?
  • If surgery is suggested, why, and what other options exist?
  • What will it cost, and what does my insurance plan cover?

NIDCR says to ask why surgery is suggested, whether other options exist, how it will help and what the risks are, so these are fair questions to ask.
