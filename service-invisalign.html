Overview

What Is
Clear Aligner Treatment?

Many patients in Oxnard search for Invisalign, which is a brand name for clear aligner treatment. Cleveland Clinic describes clear aligners as custom made, clear plastic devices used for crowded or crooked teeth, and names Invisalign as one example. Cleveland Clinic says this treatment is not an option for everyone.

Cleveland Clinic says that, unlike traditional braces, clear aligners have no metal or ceramic brackets or wire, and that you can remove them to eat, drink, brush and floss. The FDA describes clear aligners as a series of custom made, removable plastic trays designed to gradually move teeth into a desired position.

Clear aligners are one kind of orthodontic treatment. The American Dental Association's MouthHealthy site says braces and aligners are the appliances most commonly used to move teeth into position. If you are curious whether clear aligners could suit you, a dentist has to examine your teeth first. You can start that conversation with Omni Dental Specialty in Oxnard.

This page is general information and does not describe what we provide. We welcome patients from Oxnard, El Rio and College Park. You can read about our Oxnard dental office or contact us to ask a question.

What to Consider

Why Oxnard Patients Ask About Clear Aligners

01
Not an Option for Everyone

Cleveland Clinic describes clear aligners as a treatment for some mild to moderate bite problems and says they are not an option for everyone. Whether they could suit you is a question for a dentist after an exam.

02
Removable Trays

Cleveland Clinic says you can remove aligners to eat, drink, brush and floss. It also says you should store them when they are out, so ask your dentist how to handle this day to day.

03
Wearing Them as Directed

Cleveland Clinic says treatment takes discipline, because you wear the aligners as directed. Your dentist explains the wearing schedule that applies to you.

04
An Evaluation First

The FDA says to make sure you receive an evaluation by a dentist, preferably in person. It adds that in person exams can help lower risks such as an uneven bite and unintended tooth movement.

05
Mail Order Aligners

The FDA says it has not authorized clear aligners sold directly to consumers without a prescription and supervision by a dentist. The American Association of Orthodontists also warns that mail order treatment is risky without proper exams and X-rays.

06
Retainers Afterward

MouthHealthy says retainers preserve and stabilize the results of orthodontic treatment. Cleveland Clinic says stopping retainer use may allow teeth to shift back, so ask your dentist about aftercare.

The Process

How Clear Aligner Treatment Works, Step by Step

These are the general stages that dental sources describe, so patients in Oxnard know what questions to ask before any treatment begins.

Step 01
Evaluation

The FDA says treatment starts with an evaluation of your teeth and bite by a dentist, preferably in person. Your dentist can tell you whether clear aligners are an option for you.

Step 02
Photos and Impressions

Cleveland Clinic describes digital photos and tooth molds made with an optical scanner or impression material. Ask your dentist what records are taken in your case.

Step 03
Planning

Cleveland Clinic says the plan maps how each tooth will move. The FDA says digital scans or impressions are used to plan treatment.

Step 04
Making the Aligners

Cleveland Clinic says lab technicians create each aligner from the plan. The FDA describes a series of custom made aligners rather than a single one.

Step 05
Attachments and Fitting

Cleveland Clinic says the first aligner is fitted, and that small dental attachments are placed on the teeth to help movement. Your dentist tells you whether attachments apply to you.

Step 06
Check Ups and Care

Good to Know

A Closer Look at Clear Aligner Care

These general points can help Oxnard patients prepare for a visit. Your dentist explains what applies to your situation. You can also see all of our dental services in Oxnard.

  1. 01

    What your dentist reviews first

    Cleveland Clinic’s orthodontics page says people with serious issues such as extensive decay or gum disease should be treated by a dentist first. Tell your dentist about any pain or gum problems.

  2. 02

    Cleaning around appliances

    Cleveland Clinic says poor cleaning around orthodontic appliances raises the risk of cavities, so following hygiene advice matters. Ask your dentist how to clean your teeth and your aligners.

  3. 03

    Soreness while adjusting

    Cleveland Clinic says pressure or soreness is common, especially when starting each new aligner or when chewing, and typically eases within a few days. Ask your dentist what to expect.

  4. 04

    After treatment: retainers

    Cleveland Clinic describes retainers as custom appliances worn after treatment ends to prevent teeth from drifting. Ask your dentist about the wearing schedule that applies to you.

  5. 05

    Adults and clear aligners

    MouthHealthy says many adults now seek orthodontic treatment to correct long standing problems or problems caused by changes that come with maturity. Your dentist explains what applies to you.

  6. 06

    Questions to bring with you

    Write down what you want to know before you come in. The checklist further down this page groups questions by topic. Your dentist answers in plain language.

Your Options

What the Choices Can Look Like

Cleveland Clinic and MouthHealthy name several appliances used in orthodontic care. These are general descriptions only.

01
Clear Aligners

Cleveland Clinic describes custom made clear plastic aligners that replace the metal or ceramic brackets and wire of traditional braces. They are removable for eating, drinking, brushing and flossing.

02
Braces

Cleveland Clinic describes braces as brackets, bands and wires that gradually move teeth. Options include traditional metal braces and clear ceramic braces.

03
Retainers

MouthHealthy and Cleveland Clinic describe retainers as custom appliances worn after treatment to prevent teeth from drifting. Your dentist explains whether and how you would use one.

Before Your Visit

Questions to Ask at Your Consultation

Bring this list to your visit and add your own. The topics follow information shared by Cleveland Clinic, the FDA and MouthHealthy. If you want to ask something before you come to our Oxnard office, contact us.

About your teeth and bite

  • Are clear aligners an option for my teeth and bite?
  • Do my teeth or gums need attention first?
  • Which problem is treatment meant to address in my case?
  • How would my crowns, fillings or implants be affected?
  • Which other options, such as braces, should I consider?
  • Which of my medications or health conditions should we discuss?

Please do not send medical details through this website.

About treatment and aftercare

  • How should I wear my aligners and where do I keep them?
  • What should I do if an aligner is lost or damaged?
  • How will you check my progress, and how often are visits?
  • What soreness is normal, and who do I contact if something feels wrong?
  • What happens after treatment, including retainers?
  • What will it cost, and what does my insurance plan cover?

The FDA recommends periodic in person visits during treatment, so ask how follow up visits are handled.
